Your father has a check up at 10 AM in Mohakhali, your mother has a fever that will not go down, and the traffic outside your home in Mirpur is already at a standstill. Getting elderly parents to a hospital in Dhaka is hard enough without bargaining with a CNG driver, helping them climb into a high seat, or wondering whether the fever is serious enough for an ambulance.

This guide helps you decide between a car and an ambulance, prepare for the trip, and stay alert during dengue season.

## Car or ambulance: how to decide

Not every hospital visit needs an ambulance, but some clearly do. Use this as a simple guide, and when in doubt, call a doctor or choose the ambulance.

| Situation | Usually suitable |
|---|---|
| Routine check up, test or follow up visit | Car |
| Mild illness, can walk with support | Car |
| Needs a wheelchair but is stable | Car plus or micro with space |
| Chest pain, breathing trouble, fainting | Ambulance |
| Stroke signs: face drooping, weak arm, slurred speech | Ambulance |
| Heavy bleeding or a serious fall | Ambulance |
| Cannot sit up | Ambulance |

For a true emergency, do not wait. Call for an ambulance and, if possible, call the hospital ahead so they are ready.

## Making a car trip comfortable

For planned visits, a car is usually the easiest option. A few things help elderly passengers:

1. Choose a car rather than a bike or CNG. Getting in is easier and the ride is more comfortable.
2. Pick car plus or a micro if you need space for a folded wheelchair or walker.
3. Seat your parent behind the front passenger seat, so they get out on the footpath side.
4. Bring a cushion if the ride is long.

## What to bring

Keep a hospital bag ready, especially for parents with long term conditions:

1. All prescriptions and recent test reports in one folder.
2. A list of current medicines and doses.
3. National ID and any hospital card.
4. Water and a light snack, if the doctor allows.
5. A phone charger and some cash.
6. A shawl or light jacket, because hospital waiting rooms can be cold.

## Timing the trip

Dhaka traffic can turn a short trip into a long one. For planned appointments:

1. Leave early, so your parent is not rushed walking from the car.
2. Avoid the main office hours when you can.
3. Ask the hospital which gate is closest to the department you need, so the driver can drop you there.

## Dengue season: July to October

Dengue cases in Bangladesh usually rise during the monsoon months, with health authorities reporting the highest numbers between July and October in recent years. Older adults and people with other illnesses face higher risk of severe dengue.

During this period, watch closely if a parent has a fever. Health authorities and doctors advise seeking care quickly if warning signs appear, such as:

1. Severe stomach pain or repeated vomiting.
2. Bleeding from gums or nose.
3. Extreme tiredness or restlessness.
4. Breathing difficulty.

If these signs appear, treat it as urgent and consider an ambulance. For an early test after a fever starts, a car to a nearby hospital or diagnostic centre is usually enough.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I take an ambulance or a car to the hospital?

For routine visits and stable patients, a car is usually fine. For chest pain, stroke signs, breathing trouble, heavy bleeding or if your parent cannot sit up, choose an ambulance.

Car plus or a micro gives more space for a folded wheelchair and makes getting in easier.
