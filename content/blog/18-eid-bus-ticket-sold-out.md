You queued at the counter, refreshed the booking site at midnight, called every agent you know, and the answer was the same: sold out. For millions of people in Dhaka, Eid means going home, and every year bus seats for the busiest days vanish almost as soon as sales open. Standing at Gabtoli or Sayedabad two days before Eid hoping for a spare seat is stressful, expensive and often unsafe.

The good news is that a sold out bus is not the end of the road. This guide covers five realistic options, what each one suits, and how to plan so your family reaches home in one piece.

## Why Eid bus tickets disappear so fast

Bus operators sell advance tickets for a limited window before Eid, and demand on the final three or four days is far higher than the number of seats. Popular routes from Gabtoli, Sayedabad, Mohakhali and Kalyanpur fill first, especially evening departures. Counter staff sometimes sell standing space or roof seats once the official seats are gone, which is risky and often illegal.

Knowing this helps you plan. If you cannot get a seat on your preferred day, the real choice is between changing your date, changing your vehicle, or sharing a vehicle with others.

## Option 1: Travel a day or two earlier or later

The simplest fix is often timing. Seats that are impossible to find for the day before Eid can still be available a few days earlier. If your office allows it, leaving early also means:

1. Calmer highways and fewer jams at known bottlenecks.
2. Less pressure at the terminals.
3. More time with family before the busiest days.

Some families also choose to travel on the morning of Eid or the day after, when the roads out of Dhaka are noticeably quieter.

## Option 2: Book a train or launch where it fits

For some districts, the train from Kamalapur or a launch from Sadarghat is a real alternative. These tickets also sell out quickly at Eid, so check official sales dates early. They work best if you live near a station or ghat at the other end, or if you can arrange a short local ride on arrival.

## Option 3: Share a micro or Hiace with family or neighbours

If several people from the same area are heading home, a private micro or Hiace can be the most comfortable option. You leave from your own door in Mirpur, Uttara or wherever you live, carry your luggage without fighting for space, and stop when the children need a break.

Splitting the cost between a group makes this far more reachable than many people expect. We cover how to divide the fare fairly in our guide on sharing a micro or Hiace for Eid.

## Option 5: Split the journey

If nothing reaches all the way home, break the trip into parts. Take a car or micro to a major district town, then use a local bus or CNG for the last stretch. This works well for areas where buses from the district town run often but direct buses from Dhaka are full.

## Staying safe on the Eid journey

Whatever you choose, a few habits make the trip safer:

1. Never ride on a bus roof or stand in the doorway.
2. Avoid unregistered vehicles offered by strangers near the terminals.
3. Share your trip details with someone at home.
4. Check the driver and vehicle before you get in.
5. Keep cash, phone and documents in one bag you carry yourself.
