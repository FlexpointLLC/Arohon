Since MRT Line 6 opened, many Dhaka commuters have the same question every morning: take the metro, or book a ride? The honest answer is that it depends on where you start, where you end and what you are carrying. This guide compares the two on cost, time and comfort, using published metro fares and hours.

## Metro rail fares at a glance

Dhaka metro fares are set at ৳5 per km, with a minimum of ৳20 and a maximum of ৳100, as reported by Prothom Alo and The Daily Star when fares were announced. A 2023 Dhaka Tribune fare list gave these examples.

| Route | Metro fare |
|---|---|
| Shortest trip | ৳20 |
| Mirpur 10 to Motijheel | ৳60 |
| Uttara North to Motijheel | ৳100 |

Fares can be revised, so check the DMTCL notice or the ticket machine before you travel.

## Metro rail hours

According to reports in 2025, the metro runs from morning until night from Saturday to Thursday, and weekday hours were extended by one hour in October 2025, with trains starting earlier and finishing later (Dhaka Tribune, Financial Express). On Fridays, the metro runs a shorter afternoon and evening service. A January 2025 schedule put the first Friday train from Uttara North at 3:00 PM and the last from Motijheel at 9:40 PM. Check the current timetable, since hours have changed several times.

## Where the metro wins

1. **Long trips along the line.** Uttara to Motijheel, Mirpur to Karwan Bazar or Agargaon to Shahbag are all much faster on rails than on roads at peak time.
2. **Predictable timing.** Traffic does not affect the metro, so you know roughly when you will arrive.
3. **Fixed fare.** You pay the same at 9 AM as at 3 PM, with no surge and no bargaining.
4. **Rush hour.** The worse the traffic, the bigger the metro's advantage.

## Where a ride wins

1. **Your destination is far from a station.** Gulshan, Banani, Dhanmondi and Old Dhaka are not on Line 6.
2. **You have luggage or shopping.** Carrying bags through stations and crowds is hard.
3. **You are travelling as a group.** Three people in a CNG or car can cost about the same as three metro tickets plus last mile rides.
4. **Friday mornings and late nights.** When the metro is not running, a ride is your only fast option.
5. **Elderly or unwell passengers.** Door to door is kinder, and for emergencies, an ambulance booking is the right call.

## The last mile problem

The metro takes you between stations, not between your home and office. That final stretch is where most people lose time. Options:

| Last mile option | Best for |
|---|---|
| Walk | Within 10 minutes of the station |
| Rickshaw | Short hops on local roads |
| Bike ride | Solo, a few km, fast |
| CNG or car | Bags, groups, rain |

Booking a bike ride for the last mile is often the sweet spot. On Arohon, you see the fare before you book, and the driver's name, photo and plate appear before pickup, so you can step out of the station and go. Rides are cash only, so keep small notes.

## A simple decision rule

1. **Both ends near Line 6 stations?** Take the metro.
2. **One end near a station?** Metro plus a short last mile ride.
3. **Neither end near the line, or luggage, or a group?** Book a ride.
4. **Friday morning or very late?** Book a ride, since the metro may not be running.

## Tips for metro and ride commuters

1. **Get a rapid pass** if you ride the metro often, so you skip ticket queues.
2. **Set your pickup at the station gate** that faces your destination.
3. **Avoid the busiest minutes.** Trains and pickup points are most crowded right after offices close.
4. **Plan rainy days.** Waterlogged roads make last mile rides slower, so leave earlier.
5. **Share your trip at night.** Live trip sharing lets family follow your route home.
