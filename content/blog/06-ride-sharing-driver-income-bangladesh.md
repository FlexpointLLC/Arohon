If you drive for a ride sharing app, the most important number is not the fare. It is how much of that fare stays in your pocket. This guide breaks down where your money goes and shows what changes when the commission drops to 2%.

## Where a driver's money goes

On every trip, the fare a rider pays is split between:

1. **The platform's commission**, a percentage of the fare
2. **Any platform or booking fees**
3. **Your costs**: fuel or gas, maintenance, mobile data, and rent if you do not own the vehicle

Most ride apps in Bangladesh have taken **15% to 25%** commission. That is why, in 2019, thousands of drivers in Dhaka went on strike over a 25% cut and asked for 12%, and why driver unions still campaign for a cap today.

## Why low commission keeps you on the app

When a platform takes a quarter of every fare, many drivers stop using it and pick up passengers on the street instead. That feels like more money, but it comes with real risks: no trip record, no support if a passenger refuses to pay, and no rewards. With a 2% commission, the app costs you almost nothing, and you keep the protection.

## How you get paid

Rides are paid in **cash** by the rider at the end of the trip, so you have your money the moment the ride ends. The small Arohon fee is settled from the app, and you can see every trip, fee and payout in your income screen.

## Ways to earn more

- **Drive at peak times** in busy areas like Gulshan, Banani, Motijheel and Uttara.
- **Accept intercity and rental trips** for bigger fares.
- **Keep a high rating** so riders and rental customers choose you.
- **Switch to an EV bike** to cut fuel costs.
- **Refer other drivers** and earn a referral reward.
