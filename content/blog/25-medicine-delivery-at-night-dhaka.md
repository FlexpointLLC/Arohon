It is past midnight. Your child has a high fever and the paracetamol bottle is empty. Or your father's blood pressure tablets ran out and you only noticed now. Most neighbourhood pharmacies have closed their shutters, the roads are dark, and going out alone feels risky. Here are the real options for getting medicine late at night in Dhaka, in the order most families try them.

## First, is this an emergency?

Medicine can wait a few hours. Some symptoms cannot. Do not try to treat these at home with pharmacy medicine:

1. Chest pain or pressure
2. Trouble breathing
3. Sudden weakness on one side, slurred speech or a drooping face
4. Heavy bleeding
5. Fainting or not waking up properly
6. A seizure
7. Very high fever in a small baby

In these cases, go to the nearest hospital emergency or book an ambulance right away. Arohon lets you book an ambulance from the same app.

## Option 1: Hospital pharmacies

Many large hospitals in Dhaka keep their pharmacy open around the clock, because their emergency departments never close. Areas with major hospitals, such as Dhanmondi, Panthapath, Mohakhali, Shyamoli and the area around Dhaka Medical College, usually have at least one pharmacy open late.

**Good for:** urgent prescription medicine, when you can travel.
**Watch out for:** long queues at night and the trip itself, especially if you are alone.

## Option 2: Late open neighbourhood pharmacies

Some pharmacies near hospitals, clinics and busy main roads stay open late, though hours vary by shop and day. Save the numbers of two or three pharmacies near your home now, and ask them about their closing time. A quick call at night saves a wasted trip.

**Good for:** common medicine like fever, pain, gastric or allergy tablets.
**Watch out for:** the shop may be closed when you arrive, or out of the brand you need.

## Option 3: Medicine delivery apps

Ordering medicine through an app means you do not leave home or a sick family member. You enter what you need, often with a photo of the prescription, and a delivery person brings it to your door.

**Good for:** when you cannot leave the house, or for refills you know you need.
**Watch out for:** availability and delivery time can vary late at night, so order as early as you can.

## Option 4: Ask a neighbour or relative

In many Dhaka buildings, a neighbour may have a spare strip of a common medicine. It is not a long term plan, but at 2 AM it can help until morning. Never take prescription medicine meant for someone else without a doctor's advice.

## Build a simple home medicine kit

The best night time plan is not needing one. Keep these at home and check them every month:

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Paracetamol for adults and children | Fever and pain |
| Oral saline | Diarrhoea and dehydration |
| Antacid | Gastric pain |
| Antihistamine | Allergy and itching |
| Bandages, antiseptic, cotton | Small cuts and burns |
| Thermometer | Know how high the fever really is |
| A full week of regular medicine | Blood pressure, diabetes, asthma |

Write the doses for children on the box after asking your doctor. Keep the kit out of children's reach.

## Tips for refills so you never run out

1. **Refill at one week left.** Do not wait until the last tablet.
2. **Keep a photo of every prescription** on your phone.
3. **Set a phone reminder** for monthly medicine.
4. **Note the generic name**, not only the brand, so a pharmacist can offer an alternative.

## What to keep ready before you order

When you order medicine or call a pharmacy at night, have these ready so nothing is missed:

1. A clear photo of the prescription
2. The exact name, strength and quantity of the medicine
3. The patient's age, especially for children
4. Your full address with a landmark and a working phone number
