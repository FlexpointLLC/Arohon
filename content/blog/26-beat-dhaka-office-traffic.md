You leave Mirpur at 8:30 AM and reach Gulshan close to 10. In the evening it is worse. Dhaka's traffic jam is not just an annoyance, it is lost sleep, missed meetings and less time with family. You cannot fix the city's roads, but you can change when you leave, which way you go and what you ride. Small changes add up to hours every week.

## How bad is it, really?

A study by BUET (Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology) has been widely reported as estimating that traffic congestion in Dhaka wastes around 5 to 8 million working hours every day. Reports have also put the average speed of traffic during rush hour at around 5 km per hour, close to walking pace.

That means the biggest gains come from avoiding the worst hours entirely, not from finding a magic shortcut.

## The best times to leave

Rush hour in Dhaka is broad, and it shifts by area, but most commuters see a pattern like this:

| Time | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Before 7:30 AM | Roads are fairly clear, fastest morning trip |
| 8 AM to 10:30 AM | Heaviest morning rush, especially toward Motijheel, Gulshan and Karwan Bazar |
| 11 AM to 4 PM | Moderate, with jams near schools and markets |
| 5 PM to 8:30 PM | Heaviest evening rush, often worse than the morning |
| After 9 PM | Traffic eases, good time for longer trips |
| Friday | Much lighter all day |

**The simplest fix:** leave 45 minutes earlier in the morning, or stay 45 minutes later at the office in the evening. Many commuters find the trip becomes much shorter, and the time saved can go to breakfast, the gym or quiet work.

If your office allows flexible hours, ask for a 7:30 AM to 4:30 PM schedule. You beat both rushes.

## Pick the right mode for your trip

No single mode wins every route. Match the mode to the distance and the road.

| Mode | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Bike | Solo trips, heavy traffic, moderate distances | Rain, dust, and carrying bags |
| EV bike | The same trips, with a quieter ride | Same weather limits as a bike |
| CNG | Two or three people, short to medium trips | Gets stuck in jams like a car |
| Car | Comfort, meetings, rainy days, luggage | Slowest in the worst jams |
| Metro rail | Trips along its line, such as Uttara to Motijheel | Getting to and from stations |
| Walking | The last 1 to 2 km in heavy jams | Heat and broken footpaths |

A bike usually moves through jams better than a car, which is why many office workers in Dhaka commute on one. On rainy days, a car is worth the extra time.

## Smarter routes for common commutes

Each area has its own pinch points. A few practical habits:

1. **Mirpur to Gulshan or Banani.** The roads through Mirpur 10 and the cantonment area get heavy. Leaving before 8 AM makes a big difference.
2. **Uttara to Motijheel.** The metro rail can save a lot of time along this line. Take a short bike or CNG ride to the nearest station.
3. **Mohakhali and Banani.** These are busy crossing points. If you work here, a bike for the last stretch helps.
4. **Dhanmondi and Science Lab.** School hours cause local jams. Try to leave either before or after them.
5. **Gabtoli, Sayedabad and Kamalapur.** Bus terminal and railway areas are crowded at most hours, so give yourself extra time.

Check a live traffic map before you leave and be ready to switch to a different road.

## Combine modes

Some of the fastest commutes in Dhaka use two modes. For example, a bike to the metro station, the metro across the city, then a short walk to the office. Or a car in the morning when you are dressed for work, and a bike home in the evening when speed matters more.

## Use the commute time well

When the jam is unavoidable, make the time count:

1. Listen to podcasts or audiobooks.
2. Plan your day or reply to messages, if you are a passenger.
3. Keep water, a charger and a small snack with you.
4. On a bike, keep your phone away and focus on the road.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the worst time for traffic in Dhaka?

Roughly 8 AM to 10:30 AM and 5 PM to 8:30 PM on working days. The evening rush is often the heaviest.

### Is a bike faster than a car in Dhaka traffic?

Usually yes, for solo trips, because a bike moves through jams more easily. A car is more comfortable on rainy days or with luggage.

### How much time does Dhaka lose to traffic?

A BUET study has been widely reported as estimating 5 to 8 million working hours lost every day, with rush hour speeds reported around 5 km per hour.

### How can I make my office commute shorter?

Leave before 7:30 AM or after the evening rush, combine modes such as bike and metro, and pick the vehicle that fits the trip.
